Proving a chess move is the only move

1 · Definitions

What it means to prove a move is best

Engines don't prove anything. Stockfish reports “#11” after a search with heuristics, pruning and a hash table that can be wrong. A proof has to say exactly which claim it establishes, because “best move” covers several claims of very different difficulty.

The base notion is a forced win. Side a wins from position p within n moves if one of these holds: the defender is to move and is checkmated; the attacker has some legal move after which it still wins within n − 1; or the defender has at least one legal move and every one of them still loses. This is an inductive predicate, so every proof of it is a finite tree, and that is why a forced mate can be certified at all.

inductive WinsIn (a : Side) : Nat → Pos → Prop where
  | mate {n p} : p.turn = a.other → mated p = true → WinsIn a n p
  | att  {n p} (m : Move) : p.turn = a → m ∈ legal p →
           WinsIn a n (play p m) → WinsIn a (n + 1) p
  | dfd  {n p} : p.turn = a.other → legal p ≠ [] →        -- stalemate is not a win
           (∀ m ∈ legal p, WinsIn a n (play p m)) → WinsIn a n p

theorem not_both (h₁ : WinsIn a n p) (h₂ : WinsIn a.other k p) : False

not_both turns “this move wins” into “this move is optimal”. Once Black's forced win after Rxb2+ is proved, any alternative after which White has a forced win cannot also be a Black win. So the claim “Rxb2+ is the only move” needs one winning tree for Black plus one winning tree for White after each of the other 33 moves. Each claim further down this ladder needs a bigger kind of evidence:

Proved here

The move wins

∃ strategy ∀ replies …

A strategy tree: one attacker move per node, every defender reply enumerated. Its size is the sum, over the attacker's moves, of the defender's reply count, which is small when the attacker keeps giving check.

Gotham: 157 Black decisions, 315 nodes.

Proved here

It is the only winning move

win tree + a refutation per alternative

For each other legal move, a winning tree for the opponent, then not_both. This works only when every alternative loses outright. If an alternative draws, a mate tree can't refute it.

Gotham move 1: 33 refutations, longest a White mate in 5.

Harder

It is the fastest mate

¬ WinsIn a (n−1) p

Shows that no shorter mate exists. The evidence is a defender strategy that survives n−1 moves against every attacker try, so the tree branches on the attacker's ~30 moves per turn. A verified bounded search (alpha-beta with a soundness proof) fits this better than a certificate.

Not attempted. It decides whether move 8 has “duals”.

Research

The alternative only draws

¬ Wins a p, with no mate for either side

Draws have no finite strategy tree, since play can go on indefinitely. The evidence would be an invariant: a set of positions closed under the defender's replies and containing no mate (a fortress), or a retrograde tablebase computation proved correct, as Hurd did in HOL4 for four-piece endings.

Needed for move 2: 2…Rxc2+ evaluates as 0.00.

Not a theorem

It is the engine's best move

eval(m) = max eval

In positions that are neither forced wins nor provably drawn, “best” means best by a heuristic evaluation. The evaluation function isn't a mathematical object you can prove things about. You can prove facts about the search (Nipkow verified alpha-beta in Isabelle), but not that its answer is good chess.

2 · The gold-star puzzle

Rxb2+, and what is actually forced after it

The Short says Black “cannot prevent White's mate unless Black finds checkmate in 11. Everything else doesn't work.” I transcribed the position from the video, confirmed it with Stockfish, and proved the first-move version of that sentence in Lean. Step through the line below. The dot next to each Black move shows how strong a claim holds at that point.

Only move, proved in Lean Only mating move per Stockfish; an alternative isn't lost Other mates exist (duals)

The full claim “only this exact 11-move sequence works” is false as stated. At moves 8, 10 and 11 Black has other mates, slower ones or a second mate in one. In problem terms these are duals. At moves 2, 7 and 9 Stockfish finds an alternative that doesn't mate but doesn't lose either. 2…Rxc2+ scores 0.00 (a perpetual check), which is why Levy calls Rb1 “better than Rook c2”. 7…Qb5+ scores about +6 for Black with no mate inside the search horizon, so it might even be a slower forced win. That is exactly where a heuristic “best” and a provable “best” separate.

What they share

Both write the move generator as a Lean function and define checkmate as “in check with no legal moves”. Both put an untrusted oracle (Stockfish, or an LLM) next to a Lean checker, and both leave out the draw rules. Each also trusts its own move generator as the definition of chess: no proof connects it to the FIDE rulebook.

Where they differ

The difference is the quantifier. Renshaw's Opponent constructor makes you handle every defender reply, so a proof is a forced win. The hackathon pipeline checks one line in which the LLM also picks White's moves, so it confirms that a game can end in mate. That is ∃∃ rather than ∃∀. Its theorems are general facts about the rules, which Renshaw's project doesn't have.

To make that difference concrete, I fed the hackathon executable a stand-in for the LLM (the README's own mock pattern) that answers the Gotham position with a losing first move:

$ lake exe chess "2b2bk1/1r3rp1/p2p3R/q2Np2Q/4Pp1B/2p2P2/PPP4P/1K4R1 b - - 0 1" Output from LLM: {"moves":[ Qa5-b6, Rh6-h7, Qb6-b2 ]} # 1…Qb6?? 2.Rh7?? Qxb2# ================================= Puzzle Successfully Solved!

White simply had 2.Rh8#. Under the WinsIn semantics, 1…Qb6 is one of the 33 moves the kernel proved to lose.

4 · What I built

A certificate checker the kernel can run

The prototype lives in prototype/ of the PuzzleProof repository: about 650 hand-written lines of Lean with no dependencies, plus two Python scripts. The design rule is that search is untrusted and checking is verified. Stockfish never has to be right, because the kernel re-derives every legal move and every mate itself.

Move generator, tested

Legal-move counts (perft) match python-chess on the 7 standard chessprogramming positions plus the puzzle position: 3,617,785 leaf nodes in total, 0 mismatches. That is differential testing, which is evidence and not proof.

Kernel cost

One move generation costs about 0.6 s in the kernel. Running decide +kernel over the whole 315-node tree in one term filled memory until the machine swapped, and I stopped it after ten minutes. Generating one small theorem per node (320 theorems, each position written out as a literal) passes in 79 s.

Trust levels

native_decide checks the same certificate in under a second but adds Lean.ofReduceBool (it trusts the compiler). The per-node file uses only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

5 · Generating puzzles

Working backwards

The certificate view suggests making puzzles where the proof comes first and the puzzle second. A puzzle is sound in the problemist's sense when it has exactly one key move. A key that works and is unintended is a cook; an alternative later in the solution is a dual. onlyMove is a machine-checkable soundness test for the key, and you can apply it at each of the solver's turns to measure how forced a line is.

Forward: mine and certify

Lichess's puzzler scans rated games with Stockfish and calls a move an “only move” if the second-best line's win chance is more than 0.7 lower, if no second line exists, or if a tablebase says so. Replace that threshold with onlyMove and each published mate puzzle comes with a proof that its key is unique, plus a per-move forcedness profile like the dots above.

Backward: unmove from mate

Retrograde analysis starts from mated positions and un-plays moves, which is how tablebases are built. For puzzles: pick a mating pattern, un-move the attacker's last move, keep only predecessors where the attacker's move is unique, and repeat. Uniqueness is a local check at each step, and the certificate builds up as you go, so the proof grows backwards along with the puzzle.

Reachability

Renshaw's IsReachable asks whether a legal game could ever reach a position. That is the formal home for retro puzzles and proof games (“the game started normally; what was the last move?”). Such proofs are existential: one game reaching the position is the certificate. Proving a position unreachable needs invariants such as pawn structure, piece counts and bishop colors, which is the hackathon project's bishop_stays_on_colour put to real use.

Difficulty you can measure

A certificate's size and shape give an objective difficulty measure: how many defender branches there are, how many quiet (non-checking) attacker moves, how deep the longest branch goes. The Gotham tree is all checks, which is why 11 moves fit in 315 nodes. A mate in 5 with a quiet key move is usually much bushier.

6 · Beyond puzzles

What this says about programmatic chess

Proof-carrying engine output

An engine that says “#11” could emit the tree instead, and anyone could check it with a small verified checker. The checker is the only trusted code. That is the same split as SAT solvers emitting DRAT proofs, applied to game trees.

LLM proposes, Lean disposes, with the right quantifier

The hackathon's loop is the right architecture with the wrong claim. Keep the LLM as proposer, but have it propose only the attacker's moves, let Lean enumerate the defense, and check WinsIn. Failed branches show exactly which defense breaks the idea, which is useful feedback for the next proposal.

Verifiable reward signal

For training models on tactics, a kernel-checked forced mate is a reward that can't be gamed. A plausible line that only works if the defender cooperates earns nothing. That reward is binary and sparse, but for mate puzzles it is exact.

Move generators as a proof target

All three projects trust their move generator. A declarative definition of legal moves (relations, with no search) and a proof that the fast generator matches it would replace perft testing. The hackathon's lemmas about the rules are the first steps in that direction.

7 · Open threads

Next steps, ordered by payoff

  1. Tactics on top of the checker. Combine Renshaw's interactive move / opponent_move experience and its board widget with this checker, so a human can write a proof step by step and let the certificate fill in the routine branches.
  2. Remaining only-move claims in the line. Moves 1, 3, 5 and 6 are certified. Move 4 needs the same procedure with a longer refutation (White mates in up to 10). Moves 2, 7 and 9 need a proof that the alternative is not a Black win, which is a draw proof.
  3. A draw certificate. Start with a known fortress, such as king, bishop and rook pawn against king where the bishop doesn't control the promotion square. State the drawing invariant and prove it is closed under the attacker's moves.
  4. Shortest-mate proofs. A verified depth-bounded minimax that proves ¬ WinsIn a (n−1) p, with alpha-beta and transposition tables, needed to show that a mate in 11 is not secretly a mate in 10.
  5. A small certified puzzle set. Run the pipeline over a few hundred Lichess mate puzzles. Report how many have provably unique keys and how many have duals the puzzle-generation threshold missed.
  6. A legality spec. Write the rules as relations and prove the nibble-board generator correct for one piece type at a time, starting with knights.
